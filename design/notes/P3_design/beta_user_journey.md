Nadia is a negotiation student who needs practice. A year after her negotiations class, her skills have gone rusty, and she has just accepted a job offer without negotiating it at all. She wants to get back into practice, but she's in a new city and doesn't know anyone to practice with. So she makes an account on **The Whole Pie**.

In the _Bakery_, she can negotiate with an AI counterpart, with friends, or with someone new. She warms up against the AI, playing Hiro in "The Job Offer", and she turns on the prep sheet in her settings, so that before every negotiation she has to write down her target, her walkaway, and her priorities. On _Your Slice_, she reads her confidential brief, fills in her plan, and suggests negotiating for 30 minutes, which the AI agrees to at once. She negotiates under the clock, and then the _Whole Pie_ shows her what she got, and what was possible.

The AI is good practice, but Nadia wants to negotiate against a person. Her old classmate Omar lives across the country, so she picks _Friends_, chooses The Job Offer and the side of Hiro, and gets a link, which she texts him. Omar follows the link, signs up, and joins the table as Manon. While they each read their confidential briefs, Nadia suggests 30 minutes; Omar would rather have 45, and suggests that instead, and when Nadia agrees, the clock starts for both of them. They negotiate by chat and by proposals until Omar accepts a deal. The _Whole Pie_ shows each of them their own slice, and the coach writes one debrief for both of them, about what each did well and the trade they missed together. Nadia gives Omar five stars for how it was to deal with him, and her ratings in the Bakery show how much value her deals with other people have created, and how much of it she claimed.

The next week, none of her friends are free, so Nadia picks _Someone new_. Less than a minute later, someone else who wants to practice The Job Offer comes online, and the two of them land at a new table, each with a side chosen at random. Negotiating with strangers who play differently from her friends, and seeing her ratings change from one negotiation to the next, Nadia gets better at negotiating, in practice and in her next real negotiation.
