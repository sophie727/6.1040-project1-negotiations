# Confidential brief: Presley

## The situation

You are Presley, the head of product at Solidum, a big enterprise software company. Solidum is about to launch Solidum Atlas, an AI analytics assistant for enterprise operations teams that could change the company's fortunes. Sandra Reyes, the CEO, has asked you, Mandy, the head of marketing, and Sally, the head of sales, to bring her a launch plan you all agree on, and today the three of you are meeting to settle it. If you can't agree, Sandra will decide herself, and she has a reputation for plans that leave everyone unhappy.

Solidum's departments have long worked in silos, and the three of you have clashed before. Last year, Mandy announced a release date your team had never agreed to, and when your team couldn't make it, the press blamed product. Around the same time, Sally's team promised a customer a feature you never agreed to build. You're tired of other departments' ideas turning into your team's deadlines.

Six things are on the table, and all three of you know the options for each:

- **Launch date.** Engineering says Atlas could ship as early as March 1, and every extra week makes it more polished. Nobody is considering anything later than June 1. Ascend, the industry's biggest conference, is on April 15, and Mandy has been talking about launching there.
- **Halvorsen integration.** Halvorsen Logistics, Solidum's biggest customer, has been asking for an integration with its warehouse system for a year, and your team estimates it would take most of a quarter to build. It could be left out, built within 60 days of launch, or ready at launch.
- **List price.** Anywhere from $30 to $60 per seat per month.
- **Halvorsen's discount.** Anywhere from nothing to 40% off the list price. Halvorsen's contract comes up for renewal on March 31.
- **Early access.** Halvorsen could get 8 weeks of exclusive early access, every customer could join an open beta, or nobody could see Atlas before launch.
- **Design authority.** Who decides what Atlas looks like: product alone, product with marketing and sales signing off on what customers see at launch, or any of the three of you, with a veto.

## Your priorities

Control over what Atlas looks like matters most to you. You want product to decide alone. Having marketing and sales sign off on what customers see at launch would be a real loss, and giving all three of you a veto would be the worst: Atlas would be designed by committee, and nothing would ship the way it should.

Next comes the Halvorsen integration. Building it before launch would pull most of your team off Atlas itself for a quarter, so you'd rather it weren't planned at all. Building it within 60 days of launch would be manageable, and having it ready at launch would be your worst case.

Time matters just as much. Every week makes Atlas more polished, so June 1 would be best and March 1 worst, and the later the launch, the better.

You'd like real customers using Atlas before launch, so your team can fix what they find. An open beta would give you the most feedback, an exclusive early look for Halvorsen would give you some, and no early access would give you none.

The list price and Halvorsen's discount are marketing's and sales' business; they make no difference to you.

If the three of you can't agree, you expect Sandra to give you until June 1, since she knows Atlas needs time. But she'd protect the Halvorsen account, with an integration within 60 days of launch and 8 weeks of exclusive early access for Halvorsen, and, tired of the fighting, she'd give all three of you a veto over the design. You'd expect her to set the list price at $45 and give Halvorsen 20% off.

## Your points

Your score is the total points of the agreement you reach. On the launch date, values in between earn points in between: a launch on April 23, halfway between April 15 and May 1, is worth about 193 points to you.

Your BATNA, your best alternative to a negotiated agreement, is the plan you expect Sandra to impose, which is worth 800 points to you. Don't accept any agreement worth less than that: you'd be better off letting Sandra decide.
