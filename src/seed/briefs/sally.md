# Confidential brief: Sally

## The situation

You are Sally, the head of sales at Solidum, a big enterprise software company. Solidum is about to launch Solidum Atlas, an AI analytics assistant for enterprise operations teams that could change the company's fortunes. Sandra Reyes, the CEO, has asked you, Mandy, the head of marketing, and Presley, the head of product, to bring her a launch plan you all agree on, and today the three of you are meeting to settle it. If you can't agree, Sandra will decide herself, and she has a reputation for plans that leave everyone unhappy.

Solidum's departments have long worked in silos, and the three of you have clashed before. Last year, your team promised a customer a feature that Presley then refused to build, and Mandy announced a release date that product then missed. Your customers heard about both, and you were the one who had to call them.

Six things are on the table, and all three of you know the options for each:

- **Launch date.** Engineering says Atlas could ship as early as March 1, and every extra week makes it more polished. Nobody is considering anything later than June 1. Ascend, the industry's biggest conference, is on April 15, and Mandy has been talking about launching there.
- **Halvorsen integration.** Halvorsen Logistics, Solidum's biggest customer, has been asking for an integration with its warehouse system for a year, and Presley's team estimates it would take most of a quarter to build. It could be left out, built within 60 days of launch, or ready at launch.
- **List price.** Anywhere from $30 to $60 per seat per month.
- **Halvorsen's discount.** Anywhere from nothing to 40% off the list price. Halvorsen's contract comes up for renewal on March 31.
- **Early access.** Halvorsen could get 8 weeks of exclusive early access, every customer could join an open beta, or nobody could see Atlas before launch.
- **Design authority.** Who decides what Atlas looks like: product alone, product with marketing and sales signing off on what customers see at launch, or any of the three of you, with a veto.

## Your priorities

Halvorsen is your biggest customer, and keeping it through its renewal on March 31 matters more than anything else this year.

The integration Halvorsen has been asking for matters most to you. Having it ready at launch would be best, and a promise to build it within 60 days of launch would still go a long way. Leaving it out would be your worst case.

A deep discount comes next: up to 40% off would make Halvorsen's renewal easy, and the bigger the discount, the better.

You want Halvorsen to see Atlas before it renews, so 8 weeks of exclusive early access would be best. An open beta would help a little, and nothing before launch wouldn't help at all. The launch date matters about as much: the sooner Atlas ships, the sooner you can sell it, so March 1 would be best, and every week later is worth less, down to June 1, your worst case.

A lower list price makes Atlas easier to sell, so you'd prefer $30 to $60, though that matters less to you than Halvorsen's own discount. You'd also like a say in the design, so customers' requests don't get ignored: a veto would be best, signing off on what customers see at launch would be good, and leaving it all to Presley would be the worst.

If the three of you can't agree, you expect Sandra to protect the Halvorsen account, with the integration ready at launch and 8 weeks of exclusive early access for Halvorsen. But to build the integration first, she'd push the launch to June 1, and she'd keep the list price at $60 with no discount for Halvorsen, and make product get sign-off from marketing and sales on what customers see at launch.

## Your points

Your score is the total points of the agreement you reach. On the launch date, the list price, and Halvorsen's discount, values in between earn points in between: a launch on April 1 is worth about 231 points to you, and a 20% discount for Halvorsen is worth 175.
