# Confidential brief: Mandy

## The situation

You are Mandy, the head of marketing at Solidum, a big enterprise software company. Solidum is about to launch Solidum Atlas, an AI analytics assistant for enterprise operations teams that could change the company's fortunes. Sandra Reyes, the CEO, has asked you, Presley, the head of product, and Sally, the head of sales, to bring her a launch plan you all agree on, and today the three of you are meeting to settle it. If you can't agree, Sandra will decide herself, and she has a reputation for plans that leave everyone unhappy.

Solidum's departments have long worked in silos, and the three of you have clashed before. Last year, you announced a release date that Presley's team then missed, and you spent weeks apologizing to the press. Around the same time, Sally's team promised a customer a feature that Presley never agreed to build.

Six things are on the table, and all three of you know the options for each:

- **Launch date.** Engineering says Atlas could ship as early as March 1, and every extra week makes it more polished. Nobody is considering anything later than June 1. Ascend, the industry's biggest conference, is on April 15, and you have a keynote slot there if Atlas launches that day.
- **Halvorsen integration.** Halvorsen Logistics, Solidum's biggest customer, has been asking for an integration with its warehouse system for a year, and Presley's team estimates it would take most of a quarter to build. It could be left out, built within 60 days of launch, or ready at launch.
- **List price.** Anywhere from $30 to $60 per seat per month.
- **Halvorsen's discount.** Anywhere from nothing to 40% off the list price. Halvorsen's contract comes up for renewal on March 31.
- **Early access.** Halvorsen could get 8 weeks of exclusive early access, every customer could join an open beta, or nobody could see Atlas before launch.
- **Design authority.** Who decides what Atlas looks like: product alone, product with marketing and sales signing off on what customers see at launch, or any of the three of you, with a veto.

## Your priorities

The launch date matters most to you, by far. A keynote at Ascend is the best stage Atlas could ever have, and it happens only if Atlas launches on April 15. Launching early, on March 1, would at least beat Solidum's rivals to market, though without a stage, and the closer to Ascend, the better. Launching after Ascend is your worst case: a rival will own the conference, and by May 1, Atlas will look like a copy, so a launch any time from May 1 on is worth nothing to you.

You also want Atlas priced as a premium product, so the higher the list price, the better. A deep discount for Halvorsen would chip away at that story if word got out, but it's a private deal, so it matters much less to you than the list price.

You want the first time anyone sees Atlas to be on stage. An exclusive look for Halvorsen, under a nondisclosure agreement, would hurt that less than an open beta, where screenshots would spread everywhere.

After last year's missed date, you want a say in what Atlas looks like: a veto would be best, and signing off on what customers see at launch would be nearly as good. Whether Halvorsen gets its integration makes no difference to you; that's between Presley and Sally.

If the three of you can't agree, you expect Sandra, who was burned by last year's missed date, to give Presley until June 1 and full control of the design, and to leave the integration out. She does love a premium brand and a big reveal, so you'd expect her to keep the list price at $60 and give nobody early access, and to give Halvorsen 20% off to keep Sally happy.

## Your points

Your score is the total points of the agreement you reach. On the launch date, the list price, and Halvorsen's discount, values in between earn points in between: a launch on April 23, halfway between April 15 and May 1, is worth 250 points to you, and a list price of $45 is worth 200.
